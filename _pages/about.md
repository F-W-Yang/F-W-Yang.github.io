<div style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
  <h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18944">Axion Production and Detection Using a Dual NMR-type Experiment</a></h3>
  
  <div style="width: 100%; max-width: 650px; margin: 20px auto; text-align: center;">
    <img src="/images/dual_nmr_setup.jpg" alt="Dual NMR Setup" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 4px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.08);">
    <em style="display: block; font-size: 0.85em; color: #666; margin-top: 8px;">Figure 1: Schematic of the proposed dual-NMR setup featuring the spin source and spin sensor configurations.</em>
  </div>
</div>

We propose a dual NMR setup to produce and detect axions via axial current interaction. The advantages of this setup v.s. ambient axion DM experiment proposals are: 1. It can search for non-relic axions without relying on local axion density = local DM density; 2. It is a broadband search up to a maximum axion mass $$m_a\sim R^{-1}$$ beyond the Larmor frequency of the NMR device; 3. The sourced axion field is coherent with a known frequency = Larmor frequency; 4. The sensitivity of a continuous experiment for $$T_{\rm obs}$$ is equivalent to that of $$N$$ multiple experiments with a time $$T_{\rm obs}/N$$.
   
<div style="margin-bottom: 40px;">
  <h3><a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12259">Stability of Superconducting Strings</a></h3>
  
  <div style="width: 100%; max-width: 650px; margin: 20px auto; text-align: center;">
    <img src="/images/cosmic_strings_studies.png" alt="Cosmic String Studies" style="width: 100%; border-radius: 4px; box-shadow: 0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,0.08);">
    <em style="display: block; font-size: 0.85em; color: #666; margin-top: 8px;">Figure 2: Summary diagram of my work on cosmic strings across theory, phenomenology, and GW data analysis.</em>
  </div>
</div>

One of my recent papers attempts to resolve the stability problem of the fermionic superconducting strings. In this study, we provide a systematic method for computing the S-matrix for various relevant processes that govern the fate of fermion zero modes on a cosmic string background, including decay, quantum tunneling, and scattering with surrounding particles, denoted as string worldsheet formalism. After an overall understanding of these microphysics of zero modes, we find that vortons (superconducting string loops) can be stable enough to be dark matter.
